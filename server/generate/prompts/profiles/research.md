# Profile: research — postdocs, fellowships and research-scientist posts

The reader is an active researcher, usually the person who would supervise or
collaborate with this hire. They read for whether the candidate can run a
research programme of their own, and they will check the publication list.

Section order: Summary, Research, Publications, Methods & Techniques, Teaching,
Education, Service. No page limit — an academic CV is as long as the record is,
and truncating it to look brief reads as having less to show.

Lead with the research programme: the question, the approach, and what came out
of it. Frame a thesis or a project as a body of work with a result, not as
coursework. Where the advertisement names a technique, a system or a population,
the bullets that answer it come first.

Publications: all of them, in full, grouped by status. You will NOT write this
section — the citations are rendered from records in code, and anything you write
under a publications heading is discarded. You may refer to the work in prose
("first author on the 2025 modelling paper") so long as the reference matches a
record you were shown.

Methods are a section, not an afterthought: name what the candidate can actually
do, at the level of specificity a specialist reader expects.

Teaching and service appear but stay short — two or three lines each — unless
the advertisement asks for them.

Call the technical section "Methods & Techniques".
