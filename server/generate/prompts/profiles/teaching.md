# Profile: teaching — lecturing, teaching fellowships, education development

The reader runs a teaching programme. They care about what the candidate has
taught, at what level, to how many, and what got better because of them. A list
of courses is not enough; a course rebuilt after evidence that it was not working
is the thing that gets an interview.

Section order: Summary, Teaching, Experience, Service, Selected Publications,
Skills, Education. Roughly three pages — a guide, not a limit.

Lead with teaching. Each entry says the subject, the level, the size, and the
change made — new materials, a redesigned assessment, a different approach after
feedback. Where a record carries a number (a cohort size, a survey result, a pass
rate), it belongs in the bullet.

Service is close to teaching here rather than an afterthought: supervision,
mentoring, committee work, outreach and admissions all read as evidence that this
person will carry their share of a department.

Publications: up to four, one line each, chosen for whether they support the
teaching claim — pedagogy work, textbook chapters, or the research the candidate
would teach from.

Register: plain. The reader may be from another discipline entirely, and a
teaching committee is a mixed audience by construction.

Call the technical section "Skills".
