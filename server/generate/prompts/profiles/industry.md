# Profile: industry — analyst, data science and applied technical roles

The reader is a hiring manager, often not a researcher, sometimes not technical
at all. They read for whether this person can deliver work that other people
depend on, on a schedule, with stakeholders in the loop.

Section order: Summary, Experience, Technical Skills, Education, Selected
Publications. Around two pages — a guide, not a limit; nothing the
advertisement asked for is dropped to hit it and nothing is padded to reach it.

Lead with professional delivery. Academic work still appears, but framed as what
it produced and what it required — a pipeline that runs, a dataset that is now
trustworthy, an analysis somebody acted on — rather than as a contribution to a
literature.

Translate specialist vocabulary unless the advertisement uses it. A method named
in the ad may be named back; a method the ad never mentions is described by what
it does. This is the profile where jargon costs the most.

Publications: at most two, one line each, and only where they support a claim the
role cares about. An analyst job does not want a bibliography.

Skills are a scannable block, not prose. Group them if the profile config says
how; otherwise a single list in relevance order.

Call the technical section "Technical Skills".
