Write a cold email to a company that is not advertising a job.

Nobody asked for this email. That single fact decides everything about it: it
has to earn thirty seconds from someone with no obligation to give them, and it
is competing with a full inbox rather than with other applicants.

## The shape

Three or four short paragraphs, 120-180 words in total. A cold email that needs
scrolling does not get read, and length reads as "this person has not worked out
what they want".

1. **Why them, specifically.** One or two sentences that could only have been
   written to this company. Name the thing they are actually doing — from the
   notes below and nothing else.
2. **The single most relevant thing they have done.** One example, concrete, with
   the outcome. Not a summary of their career; not three examples.
3. **The ask.** Small, specific and easy to say yes to: a short call, or whether
   they are hiring for the kind of work described. Never "I would welcome the
   opportunity to discuss how I can add value".

Sign-off is added by the app. Do not write one, and do not write a greeting line
— the app writes "Hi <team>," from what it knows.

## The subject line

Plain and specific: what this is about. "Evaluation infrastructure — quick
question" beats "Application for a role" and both beat "Passionate about your
mission". No more than about eight words. Never a question mark and an
exclamation mark in the same line.

## Voice

Write as one working person to another. The register is a professional email,
not a cover letter: no "I am writing to express my interest", no "I believe my
background aligns", no third-person self-description.

Say what they did, in the past tense, in ordinary words. If the notes below show a
technical company, technical specifics are the strongest thing in the email; if
they show a small team, the fact that they have built something end to end is.

## The one hard rule about them

**Everything you say about the company must come from the notes below.** Those
notes are all anyone knows about them here — they were typed by hand, not read
off a website, and there is no advertisement behind them.

So: do not name a funding round, a customer, a product feature, a headcount or a
technology unless the notes say it. Do not claim they have used their product,
read their paper, or met anyone there. An invented detail about a small company
is caught instantly by the person who works there, and it is the one mistake a
cold email cannot survive.

If the notes are thin, say less about them and more about the work. A short
honest email beats a long one built on guesses.

## Evidence

The same rule as everywhere else: every claim about them comes from an evidence
record, cited in `evidence_ids`. The greeting, the ask and any pure framing
sentence carry no claim and need no citation.

If the notes describe work they have no record for, do not stretch one to fit. Name
what was missing in `gaps` and write the email around what is genuinely there.
